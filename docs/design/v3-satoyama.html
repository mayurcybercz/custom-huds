A Japanese hillside village that lives on your desktop. It follows your real clock and weather, and the HUD keeps to quiet rails at the edges so the scene stays the main thing. Everything on this page is a working sample: drag the time, switch to the forest, turn the sound on.

01 · a day in the village

Six moments, scripted to the clock

The scene changes with the real time of day. Each moment has its own light, people and sounds; click one to load it into the diorama above.

02 · soundscape

Twenty voices, mixed by time and place

A small director decides what plays: birds in the morning, cicadas at dusk, frogs by the creek at night, the bamboo pump only when you're near it. Almost all of it can be synthesized, which keeps the skin free of sound files like v2's rain. Two things sound much better recorded, and both have CC0 recordings available.

03 · the HUD

Widgets keep to the edges

Press HUD above to see the rails over the scene. Every widget uses one translucent colour you pick (washi, sakura, matcha or lantern), thin lines, small type and no cards stacked over the middle. The centre of the screen belongs to the village.

04 · how to build it

Three ways to draw the village

The samples use the first approach. The light works like a lantern lighting a room. The scene is drawn as if at noon, then dimmed by an ambient-light layer for the time of day. Lanterns, windows, the bonfire and fireflies add their own light back into that layer, so at night every light source lights what's around it.

07 · research notes

What the samples are based on

  • Shishi-odoshi. A bamboo tube on a pivot fills from a spout, tips to empty, and its heavy end falls back onto a rock with a sharp knock. Originally used to scare deer. Wikipedia
  • Garden soundscape reference. myNoise's Japanese Garden generator layers water, bamboo and birds much like the director here. myNoise
  • Temple bell, CC0. A bonshō recorded at Hida no Sato folk village near Takayama. Freesound · Vurca. Also a CC0 calling bell at Daitokuji, Kyoto: Freesound · nahmandub
  • Japan field recordings. RutgerMuller's pack and a night forest in Aichi. Check each file's licence.
  • Children playing. Candidates such as InspectorJ's distant playground need their licence checked; CC0 only goes in the repo.
  • Toads and kajika frogs. The common toad (hikigaeru) gives a quiet, modest "kuk-kuk"; the kajika frog of mountain streams sings a clear "fi-fi-fi", celebrated in waka poetry. Japan Times · Wikipedia · frog-call guide
  • 2D light maps. Drawing lights additively into a light layer, then multiplying it over the scene, is the standard pixel-art lighting technique. Frank Force · LutLight2D
  • Bell hours. Temples traditionally rang at dawn and dusk (ake-mutsu, kure-mutsu, about 6:00 and 18:00); many rural temples also ring at noon.
